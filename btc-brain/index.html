Forecast Scoreboard — Every Guess, Measured

Every forecast this system makes is published to an append-only public ledger before the outcome is known, then resolved automatically against the exact-horizon candle close and scored. Nothing is deleted, nothing is cherry-picked — the numbers below include every win and every loss. All figures load from the public artifacts (ledger/public/accuracy.json, signal.json, trades.json); none are typed into this page. Educational research only — not financial advice.

How to read these numbers (plain words). A probability of 0.55 means the model thinks "up" is a little more likely than a coin flip — it does NOT mean certainty. The baseline is what you'd score by always guessing the most common answer; skill only exists above that line, which is why we print it next to every hit rate. The Wilson lower bound answers "given this sample size, what's the worst this hit rate could plausibly really be?" — small samples get wide, humble bounds. And maker expectancy subtracts trading fees, because a 55% guesser can still lose money after costs. If a row doesn't clear all three bars, we say so.

    New to Bitcoin? Read this first.

    Five minutes, no jargon, no hype — what Bitcoin is, why people care, and how to use this site to actually learn.

    What is Bitcoin?

    Bitcoin is digital money that no company, bank, or government controls. Instead of a bank keeping the record of who owns what, thousands of computers around the world keep identical copies of one shared record (the blockchain), and they constantly check each other. To cheat, you would have to fool most of them at once — which is what makes the record trustworthy without needing a boss.

    Why do people care?

    Two reasons. First, scarcity: the rulebook says only 21 million bitcoins will ever exist, and nobody can change that without convincing the whole network. Regular money can be printed in unlimited amounts; Bitcoin cannot. Second, permission-free: anyone with a phone can receive or send it, anywhere on Earth, without asking a bank for an account. Whether those two properties make it valuable is exactly what the market argues about every day — that argument is the price chart.

    Why does the price jump around so much?

    Because nobody knows what it "should" be worth, the price is pure supply and demand — global, 24/7, amplified by borrowed money and emotion. Bitcoin has climbed thousands of percent and it has crashed by more than half many times (see Chapter 2 and Chapter 4). Both are normal for this asset.

    Honesty box — read this even if you skip everything else.
    • This site is for learning. It is not financial advice, and nothing here tells you to buy or sell anything.
    • If you are young: the smartest thing you can do with Bitcoin right now is understand it. Knowledge compounds and can't crash.
    • Nobody — including us — reliably predicts Bitcoin's price. Our own public scoreboard proves how hard it is: check the Forecast Scoreboard, where we publish every guess we make, including the wrong ones.
    • Never put in money you can't afford to lose completely. People who ignore this rule fund the lessons everyone else learns for free.

    Key Intelligence — The 2022 Divergence

This was a historically bullish signal — the network was growing independent of price speculation. The Federal Reserve found that LN could have reduced 2017's on-chain congestion by 93%. Long-term, LN capacity growth relative to price represents genuine adoption vs speculation, making it one of the few metrics that improves during bear markets.

    Glossary

    Bitcoin
    Digital money secured by a global network of computers instead of a bank; capped at 21 million coins ever.
    Blockchain
    The shared public record of every Bitcoin transaction, kept identical on thousands of computers that constantly cross-check each other.
    Mining
    The work of securing the network by racing to add the next block of transactions; the winner earns newly created bitcoin plus fees.
    Wallet
    Software or a device that holds the secret keys which control your bitcoin. The coins live on the blockchain; the wallet holds the keys.
    Private Key
    The secret number that lets you spend your bitcoin. Anyone who has it, has your coins — which is why it is never shared.
    Satoshi
    The smallest unit of bitcoin — one hundred-millionth of a coin. Also the pseudonym of Bitcoin's unknown inventor, Satoshi Nakamoto.
    Exchange
    A business where people trade regular money for bitcoin. Convenient, but coins left there are an IOU until withdrawn to your own wallet.
    Leverage
    Trading with borrowed money. It multiplies gains AND losses — a small move against a highly leveraged position erases it entirely.
    MVRV
    Market Value to Realized Value — compares market cap to the cost basis of all coins.
    NUPL
    Net Unrealized Profit/Loss — proportion of the network in profit vs loss.
    Puell Multiple
    Daily miner revenue relative to the 365-day average — measures miner profitability.
    Reserve Risk
    Ratio of price to hodler conviction — low values = high conviction, cheap price.
    Halving
    Block reward cut in half every 210,000 blocks (~4 years). Reduces new BTC supply.
    Death Cross
    50-day MA crossing below the 200-day MA. Often a lagging indicator in BTC.
    Golden Cross
    50-day MA crossing above the 200-day MA. Historically bullish signal.
    DCA
    Dollar Cost Averaging — systematic periodic buying regardless of price.
    Kelly Criterion
    Optimal bet sizing formula based on win rate and payoff ratio.
    Fear & Greed Index
    Composite sentiment metric (0=extreme fear, 100=extreme greed).
    MACD
    Moving Average Convergence Divergence — momentum and trend indicator.
    RSI
    Relative Strength Index — oscillator measuring overbought/oversold conditions.
    Bollinger Bands
    Price envelopes 2 standard deviations from the 20-day SMA.
    DXY
    U.S. Dollar Index — measures dollar strength against a basket of currencies.
    Drawdown
    Peak-to-trough decline from an all-time high. Measures downside risk.
    HODL
    Hold On for Dear Life — long-term holding strategy regardless of volatility.
    On-Chain
    Data derived directly from the blockchain (transactions, addresses, flows).
    Spot ETF
    Exchange-traded fund holding actual Bitcoin, providing regulated institutional access.
    Black Swan
    Unpredictable, high-impact event that retrospectively appears inevitable.
    Volatility Regime
    Market phase defined by realized volatility levels (low, medium, high, extreme).
